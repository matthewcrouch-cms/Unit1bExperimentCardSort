Experiment A: A personal fitness coach wants to see if weight training or cardio is more effective in contributing to weight loss. Ninety volunteers are randomly assigned using a random number generator to one of three programs: weight training, cardio training, no training. The weight training program and cardio training program each consist of 3 workouts per week, while the “no training” program consists of no workouts. Participants will do each program for 6 weeks, and then weight loss will be recorded.

Experiment B: A web designer wants to test how user-friendly the new design is. He will have volunteers complete a set of tasks on either the old website or the new website, then record the time it takes to complete. A lower time indicates that the website is well designed and user friendly. He is concerned that age may have an impact on the times, since younger adults tend to be more technologically adept. Therefore, he plans to separate the volunteers by age first, and then randomly assign half of the age group to complete the tasks on the new webpage and the other half of the age group to complete the tasks on the old webpage. The volunteers will not be told which web page is the “new” or “old” design. The responses will be compared within each age group.

Experiment C: Artificial turf is a synthetic playing surface designed to resemble natural grass. An experiment will be implemented to analyze the mean difference in sprint time between running on grass versus artificial turf. One hundred volunteer football players will sprint 40 yards on both surfaces. Volunteers will all receive the same warm up and wear the same shoe brand. The surface they sprint on first will be randomly determined using a coin flip. The differences in their times (grass - artificial turf) will be recorded.
